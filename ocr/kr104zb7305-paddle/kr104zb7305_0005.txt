Habitat C (530 square meters) is the relatively
unprotected area between Seal and Bird Rocks. Surge and
swell increase fairly dramatically as one moves into this
habitat; waves are often breaking just south of Bird Rocks.
The depth here is 10 to 15 feet; it contains the sparse edges.
of the kelp forest and some Phyllospadix covered rocks. I
spent 10 minutes observing this area.
Habitat D (730 square meters), the area protected by
Bird Rocks, is marked on its southern boundary by Pete's
Rock. It is a shallow area of approximately 5 feet in depth.
with parts being too shallow to snorkel at low tide. The
bottom of the southern side is light-colored and sandy, while
Phyllospadix grows thickly in the northern section. Although
this habitat is protected from breaking waves, the water
currents can be very strong as the water moves through the
channel in one direction to the south. Observation time here
ranged from 10 to 15 minutes.
Habitat E (570 square meters) is the channel beyond Bird
Rocks that leads into a more exposed section of Monterey Bay.
Phyllospadix is heavy, but as the area slopes down to its
maximum depth of 15 feet; the surfgrass divides to line the
edges of the sandy channel. The channel opens up onto some
scattered Macrocystis plants which mark the end of my study
area. This is the most violent water of my study: water
