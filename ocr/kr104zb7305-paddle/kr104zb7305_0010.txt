The rainbow surfperch were the least dense of the five
primary Embiotocids in the study area. They were observed in
greatest density in the exposed habitat between Bird and Seal
Rocks in schools of 10 to 20 on the sandy bottom. The same
behavior was observed in the area around the railroad tracks
and on the south edge of the Seal Rocks habitat on the border
of the sand channel. I often observed rainbow surfperch
feeding by picking at the algal turf of the sandy ocean
floor. H. Caryi were rarely seen outside of their schools as
individuals. I observed two noticably pregnant rainbow
surfperch.

Reef Surfperch (Micrometrus aurora)
The most dense Embiotocid in the entire study area was
seen primarily in the area protected by Seal Rocks. A
statistically significant correlation was found between the
number of reef surfperch in this habitat and the height of
the tide: the lower the tide, the greater the number of fish
inhabiting this area. (See figure 6) During low tides, the
reef surfperch were observed in large schools of 50 to 100 in
the shallow, sandy, nearshore section. Several times I
noticed the presence of these schools while standing on the
rocks next to the water at low tide.
