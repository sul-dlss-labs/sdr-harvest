ABSTRACT

To examine what factors influence surfperch distribution
and behavior, I made observations of a protected nearshore
area off Point Cabrillo, California at different times of the
day and tidal cycle. Within the study area, there was
substantial niche diversity in the members of the family
Embiotocidae among different habitats of similar depths. The
black surfperch were found in the most protected area of the
study and in highest densities at low tide. The striped and
pile surfperch were equal in abundance in all habitats. The
rainbow surfperch were seen in small schools on sandy
bottoms. The reef surfperch were the most abundant species
of Embiotocid: they inhabited a shallow nearshore region and
concentrated in large schools at the entrances to channels
during low tides.
Niche diversification seems to be a relevant way of
viewing dynamic fish distributions and an important factor to
be considered in choosing methods for conducting population
studies.

INTRODUCTION

Embiotocidae (surfperch) is a viviparous fish family
found only in the North Pacific and fairly abundantly in
Monterey Bay. A diverse population occurs in the protected
nearshore area of Point Cabrillo. As with other viviparous
animals, the young are nurtured internally by their mother.
