The rainbow surfperch, though not as abundant as the
other four species, were consistently found in small schools
on sandy bottoms in depths of ten or more feet. Although
they did not occur in the exposed channel past Bird Rocks
which does have a sandy bottom, they appeared to be
successful in the rough but less-exposed area between Bird
and Seal Rocks. Perhaps the rainbow surfperch can live
successfully in schools on any sandy bottom so long as the
surge or rough water is under some critical point.
The most dynamic population of Embiotocids was the reef
surfperch: depending on the height of the tide, their
numbers ranged from zero to 250. Figure 6 suggests that
there could exist some critical tidal level such as 1.5 feet,
under which the large schools of reef are always in the Seal
Rocks habitat. As with the black perch, I have only
speculative insight into the high-tide habitat of the
Micrometrus aurora: they could be broadly spread through the
complex rocky shallow water habitat.
