These fish have a gestation period of approximately five to
six months (Burgess and Axelrod 1984), ending in the
springtime. Live-bearing is an evolutionary adaptation to
reduce early direct mortality; thousands less eggs are
fertilized yearly.
Behavioral and dietary studies have been conducted in on
two congeneric species, E. jacksoni and E. lateralis. Hixon
(1980) observed competitive interactions between the two
species in Santa Barbara and found the striped surfperch to
be the dominant competitor over the shallow algae-covered
reef, leaving the black surfperch to forage over deeper turf.
Similar studies at Hopkins Marine Station by Tom (1988)
reported that the black and striped surfperch there share
their available prey resources and do not differ
significantly in distribution.
Observations made by Nichols (1979) of the surfperch in
a 20 square meter transect in the same area as my study
reported sharpnose and shiner surfperch, both found in
schools, to be the most abundant Embiotocids, followed by
pile, black striped, and calico surfperch. A personal
communication by C. Baxter reported large schools of shiner
surfperch from 1974 to 1988 and an abundance of dwarf
surfperch from 1985 to 1987.
