An interesting and statistically significant correlation
was discovered between the number of black surfperch under
the railroad tracks and the height of the tide. (See figure
5) As the tide rises, the number of E. jacksoni beneath the
tracks decreases.
I usually found these fish concentrated in tight groups
of about 15 under each of the algae covered railroad ties and
maintaining a pacing behavior back and forth to either side
of the ties. Along with the relatively calm water in this
habitat, the tracks seemed to afford these perch a protected
area and a large, open, horizontal plane of water to inhabit.
They were all adults (20 to 30 cm in length); I once saw a
large black chase a smaller black from beneath a railroad
tie. I recognized four pregnant females and one juvenile. I
witnessed black surfperch feeding both by picking and
winnowing. On seven sightings throughout the study I
observed E. jacksoni moving in odd manners: quivering and
flipping to one side seemingly scratching their dorsal fins.
Interestingly, I saw higher numbers of black surfperch
than usual in the area protected by seal rocks during my two
night snorkels. They seemed less active nocturnally than any
other species.
